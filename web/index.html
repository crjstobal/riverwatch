A camera that watches the low-water crossing for you

Over half of flood drownings happen when a vehicle is driven into flood water, and low-water crossings are where it happens. RiverWatch reads the water level from existing USGS river cameras with OpenCV 5, cross-checks it against official gages, and an Amazon Nova agent decides what to do next: look more often, flag a broken sensor, or ask a human to close the crossing. Everything runs on AWS (Lambda on Graviton, S3, CloudFront, Bedrock, SNS).

      Where it fails

        How it works

        1. Perceive (OpenCV 5). Quality gate (infrared night mode, blur, fog, contrast), ECC registration against the calibrated view using only static structures, flowing-water mask from local normalized cross-correlation between frames 15 minutes apart, waterline as the median top edge of the water body across the channel, monotone calibration to stage.
        2. Decide (Amazon Nova 2 Lite). The agent reads the camera, can look at the annotated frame itself, compares the official gage and the upstream gage, and chooses the next check interval.
        3. Act, with a human in the loop. Raising the public alert needs an operator. Guardrails in code: two independent confirmations to escalate, no lowering while a recent reading still implies a higher level.

        Data: USGS HIVIS cameras and NWIS gages (public domain). Thresholds: NWS "Turn Around, Don't Drown" guidance (6 inches of fast water can knock over an adult, 12 inches can carry away most cars, 2 feet can carry away SUVs and trucks).

        RiverWatch, OpenCV AI Competition 2026. Research prototype: not an official warning service. Always follow local authorities.
